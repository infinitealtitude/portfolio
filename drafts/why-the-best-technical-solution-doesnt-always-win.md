# Why the best technical solution doesn't always win

*Draft for review*

I spent more than 14 months on an enterprise opportunity that involved roughly 50 to 60 stakeholders. We invested time in technical and commercial discussions. The customer ultimately selected a competitor.

My instinct was to look for the missing technical argument. A better question was whether we had given enough people confidence in the decision. A demonstration can show that a workflow is possible. A relevant reference helps a buyer judge whether a supplier can deliver it in an organisation like theirs. Clear positioning helps an internal sponsor explain the recommendation to people who never saw the demo.

I took three practical lessons from that pursuit. Find out what risk each stakeholder is accountable for. Ask what evidence would make the recommendation easier to defend. Check whether the people making the final decision can explain the case in their own words.

I can’t know how every stakeholder weighed the options. What I can carry forward is a broader definition of technical selling. The job includes proving the solution and helping the customer make a decision they can stand behind.
